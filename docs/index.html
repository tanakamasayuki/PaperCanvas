What this does

Lay out a receipt or a label, see exactly what the printer will produce, and take away a C++ header you paste into your sketch. Fill in the data struct, call the build function, print.

The preview is not an approximation. Glyphs come from lgfx-font-tool, which is verified against the real LovyanGFX, and the layout rules are compared against PaperCanvas itself in the library's test suite.

Start

Receipt — fixed width, grows with its content, and has a table whose rows repeat for however many items you pass at run time.
Label — a fixed canvas with everything placed by rectangle.

Before you start: the printable width

This one number matters more than anything else. Too small and you waste paper; too large and the printer cuts the right edge — and nothing tells you it happened.

It is normally in the printer's spec in dots, not millimetres, because the printable area is narrower than the paper. Converting 58 mm at 203 dpi gives 463 dots, but nearly every 58 mm printer prints 384. Use the number the manufacturer publishes.

58 mm384 dots
80 mm576 dots

Images

Pictures are reduced to 1bpp here, in the browser, and embedded in the generated header as a byte array. The device prints those bits unchanged, so what you see in the preview is what comes out — there is no second reduction on the device to disagree with the first.
